Homies · Task Management for Neurodivergent Users

Redesigned the core task management and progress tracking experience of a housing platform connecting neurodivergent individuals with supportive caregiver roommates, making daily routines organized, motivating, and personalized.

The Problem

Homies had the right vision but the original dashboard felt like a maze. There was no task flexibility, no personalization, and no meaningful difference between the neurodivergent user experience and the caregiver experience, despite their completely different needs, mental models, and daily contexts.

Research Findings · 7 User Interviews
70%
wanted more clarity and transparency when selecting a compatible roommate
60%
needed a flexible, real-time system that adapts to forgotten or changed tasks
50%
wanted personalization features for tasks, icons, colors, and profile content
40%
struggled with visual clarity and needed more accessible, readable features
"I want more color, visuals and personalization in the app."
Santiago, Homie (user interview)
What I Designed
1
Two separate interface experiences within one cohesive product: a motivation-first view for neurodivergent users with star rewards, color-coded status tags, and a "Needs Attention" tab; plus an efficiency-first caregiver dashboard with a pending approval modal and dual-tab task management system
2
Simplified two-step task creation that stripped required fields back, made optional inputs visibly optional, and added customizable icons, reminders, and personalized feedback messages, resulting in a 20% faster task creation rate
3
Progress tracking with weekly and monthly calendar views, a visual progress bar, and a "Share My Progress" social feature to build accountability and long-term motivation for users managing independent daily routines
4
Full design system built from scratch including the complete color palette, avatar system, task card components, error and success states, and a developer-ready style guide enabling consistent implementation across the team
Why This Project Matters
Homies is a platform connecting neurodivergent individuals with supportive caregiver roommates. I redesigned the entire task management system. What made this interesting was that I had two completely different users inside one product with fundamentally different needs. So I ran 7 interviews across both groups and designed separate interfaces for each. I also built the full design system from scratch. We projected a 30% increase in task completion and 40% improvement in retention. This felt very relevant to government work because I was designing for an underserved population who often gets overlooked in tech.
Project 02

Aspire Education · Nonprofit Website Redesign

Rebuilt the website of a tutoring nonprofit from the ground up, designing completely separate user journeys for three distinct audiences within one accessible, mobile-first, scalable design system.

The Problem

Aspire Education had one website trying to serve three completely different people, and the result was it served none of them well. Students and families could not find tutors. Volunteer tutors had no clear onboarding path. Donors could not see the organization's impact. Three confused audiences, zero clear journeys, and a nonprofit that could not communicate its value to anyone.

The 3 Users I Designed For
Students and Families
Clear navigation, plain language, and simple next steps designed for varying digital literacy levels and primarily mobile access
Volunteer Tutors
Streamlined onboarding, expectation-setting, and session management with minimal friction from discovery to first session
Donors
Impact storytelling, clear metrics, and a trust-building donation flow designed to convert interest into lasting commitment
Design Approach
1
Separate information architectures per audience so each user type has a distinct entry point, navigation path, and calls to action, eliminating the cognitive load of a one-size-fits-all homepage that confused everyone
2
Accessibility-first design throughout including WCAG compliance, high-contrast color system, readable typography, and plain-language content hierarchy for users across ability and digital literacy levels
3
Modular, reusable design system with a comprehensive component library and documented style guide, reducing projected development time by 40% and allowing the nonprofit's small team to maintain and grow the site independently
4
Mobile-first responsive design built around the primary device of the families being served, not as an afterthought, ensuring the experience worked for people with limited desktop access
Designing public-facing services for audiences with different digital literacy, different devices, and different goals is exactly what government UX requires. This project translates directly to civic technology work.
Why This Project Matters
Aspire Education is a tutoring nonprofit. The challenge was that three completely different people used the same site: students and families, tutors, and donors. I designed three separate user journeys within one cohesive site. I also built a modular design system that reduced development time by 40%, which matters a lot for a nonprofit with limited resources. And I was focused on accessibility throughout because a lot of the families being served were not highly tech-savvy, which I think is exactly the kind of thing that matters in government services too.
Project 03

The Ninety-Nines · Women in Aviation Web Platform

Designed the end-to-end UX for the "Let's Fly Now!" program launch on The Ninety-Nines' new website, an international nonprofit promoting women in aviation, enabling event discovery and role-based registration for three distinct participant types.

The Problem

The Ninety-Nines was launching their very first flight program with no existing digital infrastructure, no design precedent, and a complex backend controlled by a third-party developer with restricted access. We needed to design a compelling, public-facing experience from zero that worked seamlessly with a backend system we had limited visibility into.

Who I Designed For
Passengers
First-time flyers discovering the program, browsing upcoming events, and completing a simple sign-up with confirmation
Pilot Volunteers
Experienced pilots completing logistics forms, liability documentation, and submitting details to chapter leadership
Ground Volunteers
Event supporters selecting their participation role, registering simply, and receiving clear confirmation
Chapter Leaders
Internal admins initiating events through a structured creation flow that feeds the public-facing event listings
My Process
1
Mapped the full system before designing anything by documenting the 3-tier event lifecycle from chapter leader to super admin to public users in Figma, ensuring every design decision was grounded in how the backend data actually flowed
2
Low-fidelity wireframes reviewed with the client to validate structure and functionality before investing in any visual design work, keeping the process lean and feedback-driven
3
High-fidelity interactive Figma prototype covering all user roles, role-based registration forms, liability and pilot documentation, and full confirmation flows
4
8 functional requirements scoped and delivered including public event pages, role-based sign-up forms, liability forms, confirmation emails, duplicate registration prevention, location lookups, and end-to-end testing with sample events
Why This Project Matters
The Ninety-Nines is an international nonprofit promoting women in aviation. They were launching their first-ever flight program and needed a full web experience built from scratch. I designed the end-to-end UX for the entire platform, including event discovery and three separate role-based registration flows for passengers, pilots, and volunteers. Before I designed a single screen, I mapped the entire backend event lifecycle, because I needed to make sure what I designed would actually work with the system. This was really about systems thinking, which I think translates directly to how government digital services are built.
